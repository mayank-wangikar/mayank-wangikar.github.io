Visualizing a Vertical Decomposition on the Real Line July 02, 2026▶

In the summer of 2026, I'm doing my internship on topological groups and their representations, following K. Chandrasekharan's A Course on Topological Groups. While working through the proof of Theorem 3—that there exists a regular measure $m$ on $R$ such that for $f \in C_0$,

$$P(f) = \int_R f(x)\, dm(x),$$

where the integral is taken over the measure space $(R, \mathcal{S}, m)$, with $\mathcal{S}$ the Borel ring on $R$ and $R \in \mathcal{S}$—I needed to establish the inequality $f \in C_0,\ f \geq 0 \implies P(f) \geq \int f\, dm$, where $C_0$ is the space of real continuous functions with compact support (i.e. functions vanishing outside some compact subset of $R$).

Since $R$ here is a locally compact Hausdorff group, and $\mathbb{R}$ is the simplest example of one, that's the setting I used to explore the idea.

The book's proof begins by decomposing $f$ where $0 \leq f \leq 1,$ for all $x \in R$. This follows:

$$f(x) = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n} f_i(x),$$

where $n$ is a given positive integer, and for $i = 1, 2, \dots, n$,

$$ f_i(x) = \begin{cases} 0, & \text{if } f(x) < \dfrac{i-1}{n}; \\[6pt] n f(x) - (i-1) = \dfrac{f(x) - \frac{i-1}{n}}{\frac{1}{n}}, & \text{if } \dfrac{i-1}{n} \le f(x) \le \dfrac{i}{n}; \\[6pt] 1, & \text{if } \dfrac{i}{n} < f(x). \end{cases} $$

I had two questions reading this: why decompose $f$ this way at all, and what does the decomposition actually look like? So I sketched the $f_i$ by hand for small $n$ (2 or 3), trying a few simple functions as $f$. Then I tried for $f(x) = \frac{1}{4}(4-x^2)$ for $x \in [-2,2]$ and $0$ otherwise. It happended that these decomposition fuctions, looked like the simple functions I started with.

So I built a small interactive tool to visualize it directly: pick an $f$, pick $n$, and watch the $f_i$ layers assemble back into $f$ as you toggle them on and off.
